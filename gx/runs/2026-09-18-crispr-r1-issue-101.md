Found by the `oligolia-gx-tests` differential round on CRISPR (round 1). Not fixed here — this is a report, with a sealed failing test attached.

## Symptom

The CRISPR panel's off-target checkbox says, in its own tooltip
(`gui/panels/crispr_panel.py:74-77`):

> Scan the target sequence for near-match sites and score guide specificity (MIT).

The MIT score (Hsu et al. 2013) exists to say *where* a mismatch is. A mismatch in
the PAM-proximal seed all but abolishes cutting; a mismatch at the PAM-distal 5'
end barely matters. The app reports the **same number, 50.0**, for both.

Whatever scale one thinks is right, a seed mismatch and a PAM-distal mismatch
cannot be the same number under a score called MIT.

## What is pinned, what is re-typed, and why the name is part of the defect

- **Pinned bytes:** `gx/corpus/crispr/crispor-v3.1.py` (CRISPOR v3.1), sha256
  `aaa4d8f362af255f76127e58b5838e8ee199db533b4a228448811cc45b5efa37`. It contains
  the three definitions this issue turns on: `hitScoreM` (the 20 Hsu weights),
  `calcHitScore`, and `calcMitGuideScore`.
- **Re-typed, not imported:** `gx/crispr/crispr_oracle.py` transcribes
  `hitScoreM` → `HIT_SCORE_M`, `calcHitScore` → `mit_hit_score`,
  `calcMitGuideScore` → `mit_guide_score`. The CRISPOR files are reference bytes
  under a contested licence (`gx/corpus/MANIFEST.json` records the conflict: the
  v3.1 tag's LICENSE.txt says CC BY-NC 3.0, master's says those files were GPLv3 up
  to and including 3.1), so they are pinned beside the oracle and never imported.
  `crispr_oracle._assert_against_pinned_bytes()` runs at import time and fails the
  whole suite if a re-typed number has drifted from the pinned copy — so the
  transcription cannot silently rot, and deleting both `.py` files leaves a working
  oracle and loses only the byte-level pin.
- **The name is part of the defect.** `backend/crispr_offtarget.py` cites
  "MIT-style specificity score (Hsu et al. 2013)" in its module docstring, carries
  that paper's 20 weights verbatim in `MIT_WEIGHTS` (which compares **equal** to the
  pinned `hitScoreM`), and the GUI surfaces the result in a column headed
  "Specificity" with a tooltip that says MIT. The weights are then never applied to
  real data. The number a user reads is not a weak MIT score; it is a function of
  the mismatch-bucket counts alone, wearing the name of a position-dependent model.
  A fix that changed the arithmetic but kept the label, or kept the arithmetic and
  dropped the label, would each address the defect; shipping both the citation and
  the collapsed computation is what makes it one.

## Requirement

**CR-11** (`gx/crispr/register.json`):

> `specificity_score` is presented to the user as an MIT score — the CRISPR panel's off-target checkbox reads 'Scan the target sequence for near-match sites and score guide specificity (MIT)' — and backend/crispr_offtarget.py cites Hsu et al. 2013 and carries that paper's 20 position-specific weights. The defining property of that score is position dependence: a mismatch in the PAM-proximal seed (protospacer position 20, weight 0.583) is far more damaging to an off-target's viability than a PAM-distal one (position 1, weight 0.000). The app's specificity_score must therefore differ between two references that place a guide's single 1-mismatch off-target at those two positions. Pinned from the transcribed definition: hit score 41.7 and Sguide 70.6 for the position-20 mismatch, hit score 100.0 and Sguide 50.0 for the position-1 mismatch.

## Differential evidence

Exact input, from the register's CR-11 parameters:

- guide: `ACGTACGTAAGGCCTTGACA` (20 nt), `cas_family="cas9"`, `max_mismatches=3`
- carrier for each reference: `TTTTTT` + 20 nt protospacer + `AGG` + `TTTTTT`
  — one NGG site per reference, flanks that form no second site
- references: `[carrier(guide), carrier(off_target)]`, where `off_target` is the
  guide with exactly one base substituted

| mismatch at | Hsu weight | oracle hit score | oracle Sguide (pinned) | **app `specificity_score`** | app `summary` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| protospacer position 20 (PAM-proximal seed) | 0.583 | 41.7 | **70.6** | **50.0** | `{'0': 0, '1': 1, '2': 0, '3': 0}` |
| protospacer position 1 (PAM-distal) | 0.000 | 100.0 | 50.0 | 50.0 | `{'0': 0, '1': 1, '2': 0, '3': 0}` |

Note the two `summary` dicts are byte-identical — the app cannot tell these two
references apart, because the only thing it kept from the scan is *how many*
mismatches each hit had.

**The aggregate is not the problem.** `backend/crispr_offtarget.py:149` computes
`100.0 / (1.0 + hit_score_sum)` with hit scores in [0,1]; the pinned
`calcMitGuideScore` computes `100/(100+hitSum)*100` with hit scores in [0,100].
Those are the same function. Feed the app's aggregate the pinned position-20 hit
score (0.417 in its scale) and it returns 70.57. The entire discrepancy is in the
per-hit score.

## Where it is

`backend/crispr_offtarget.py`:

- `:130` — `mm = _mismatch_positions(guide, candidate)` computes the real 1-indexed
  mismatch positions…
- `:132` — …and `summary[str(len(mm))] += 1` throws them away, keeping only the count.
- `:143-145` — the bucket is then re-expanded into **synthetic** positions:
  ```python
  # Approximate: treat all hits in a bucket as the worst case for that
  # bucket (mismatches in the most-tolerant PAM-distal positions).
  positions = list(range(1, int(n_mm_str) + 1)) or []
  ```
  Positions `1..n` are the zero-weight PAM-distal end (`MIT_WEIGHTS[0..4]` are
  `0.000, 0.000, 0.014, 0.000, 0.000`), so `t1` in `_mit_hit_score` collapses to ~1.0
  and a single-mismatch off-target is scored as though it were a perfect on-target
  match. The comment calls this the "worst case"; it is the worst case for the
  *reported number*, i.e. the most permissive possible off-target, which is the
  opposite of conservative from the user's point of view.
- `:17-22` — `MIT_WEIGHTS`, correct and unused on real data.

## A second bug in the same function, latent until this one is fixed

`backend/routers/crispr.py:196` passes `cas_family="cas12a"` for AsCas12a, and
`scan_off_targets` (`backend/crispr_offtarget.py:122-125`) then uses the Cas12a PAM
finder but the **unchanged SpCas9 weight vector**. For Cas12a the PAM is 5' of the
protospacer, so index 19 is PAM-**distal** — the exact reverse of the seed
`MIT_WEIGHTS` encodes. Fixing the bucket approximation without also handling the
Cas12a orientation would trade one wrong answer for another. This is not separately
testable while positions are ignored, which is why it is recorded here rather than
in its own issue.
